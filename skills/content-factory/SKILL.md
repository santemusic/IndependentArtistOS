# Content Factory Operations

## Mission
Build a repeatable content engine that converts artist identity, releases, live moments and stories into high-quality platform-native assets with measurable purpose and controlled approvals.

## Stage 0 — Intake
Required: Goal ID, Project/Release ID, campaign objective, target audience, positioning/story, CTA, deadlines, channels, approved artist/brand guardrails, source assets, budget/capacity constraints and approval owner. Missing mandatory information → NEEDS_INPUT.

## Stage 1 — Content architecture
Define content jobs: discovery, identity, narrative, proof, participation/community, conversion and retention. Build content pillars and recurring series. Assign platform roles. Define format mix and production cadence based on objective and capacity rather than arbitrary posting quotas.

## Stage 2 — Idea system
Generate ideas from: song moments/lyrics, artist story, process/BTS, performance, personality/worldview, fan questions, cultural context, collaborations, live moments, transformations, education/explanation, participation prompts and campaign milestones. Rank ideas by strategic relevance, originality, expected attention/conversion value, production effort and reusability.

## Stage 3 — Creative direction
Create campaign world: emotional objective, visual vocabulary, references, motifs, setting, styling, framing, lighting, graphic language, hero moments and anti-reference/prohibited clichés. Keep the work original and consistent with approved artist identity.

## Stage 4 — Brief
Every production asset or batch should specify: Content ID, objective, audience, platform, format, hook, core idea, CTA, script/copy, shot requirements, source audio, dimensions/duration, deadline, owner, reviewer, approval owner and definition of done.

## Stage 5 — Script/copy
Create hook variants when testing is useful. Scripts should be speakable and factually grounded. Separate artist-confirmed personal facts from creative suggestions. Captions/CTAs should match campaign phase. Verify dates, names, lyrics, credits and claims.

## Stage 6 — Pre-production
Create shot list, production schedule, location requirements, people/crew, equipment, wardrobe/props, audio/lighting, permissions/releases, contingency plan, storage/media plan and capture checklist. Batch compatible concepts to maximize usable assets per setup.

## Stage 7 — Production
Capture hero requirement first, then priority variants and reusable B-roll/details. Confirm audio/image quality during production. Track captured/missing shots. Back up source media according to project policy before treating production as complete.

## Stage 8 — Post-production
Ingest → organize → selects → rough cut/design → internal review → revision → artist review where required → final QA → platform derivatives → publish-ready export. Maintain version naming and avoid feedback ambiguity.

## Stage 9 — Platform adaptation
Adapt intentionally for context, not merely crop. Check aspect ratio, duration, safe zones, caption placement, thumbnail/cover, opening frame, sound, CTA, title/description and platform-specific metadata. A derivative should have a reason to exist.

## Stage 10 — QA gate
Before PUBLISH_READY verify: correct asset/version, no placeholder text, correct artist/song names, correct dates/links, captions/spelling, visual/audio integrity, platform dimensions, approved creative state, rights/usage status known, no unintended private/sensitive material, CTA correct and final location registered.

## Stage 11 — Asset library
Each asset record should include Content ID, Goal/Project/Release ID, title, source, asset type, creator, version, platform, dimensions/duration, creative status, rights status, approval owner, final path/link, derivatives and publish/performance references where available.

## Stage 12 — Publishing handoff
Content Factory normally hands PUBLISH_READY assets to Growth/Release. The handoff includes final asset, caption/copy, platform, desired publish window, CTA, campaign/experiment ID, rights/approval status and any posting notes. Do not claim publication unless the publishing system confirms it.

## Stage 13 — Performance loop
Data/Growth returns observed performance by content/creative identifier. Content Strategist identifies patterns in hook, format, topic, length, visual, CTA and audience response. Separate correlation from causation. Convert strong patterns into new hypotheses, not permanent dogma.

## Production economics
Track high-cost hero assets separately from low-cost recurring content. Favor reusable capture, batch production and derivative systems where they preserve quality. Measure output as usable approved assets and business/learning value, not hours spent or raw footage volume.

## Human gates
Artist review is required for sensitive personal stories, identity-defining creative, material public-positioning changes and any explicitly designated hero asset. Legal/Clearance review is required where third-party rights, releases, trademarks, locations or uncertain usage rights are material.
