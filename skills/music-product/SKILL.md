# Music & Product Operations

## Mission
Move music through a controlled creative pipeline while preserving artistic authority, rights information, source assets and release readiness.

## Lifecycle
IDEA → WRITING → DEMO → DEVELOPMENT → PRODUCTION → RECORDING → EDIT/COMP → MIX → MASTER → PRODUCT_QA → RELEASE_READY → RELEASED → CATALOG

## 1. Song intake
Create a Song ID. Capture working title, composition status, recording status, contributors, source location, artist intent, references, priority, target project/release if known and unresolved rights issues.

## 2. A&R gate
Assess strategic/creative fit, emotional impact, differentiation, memorable elements, performance strength and development gaps. Feedback is advisory; Artist retains creative authority.

## 3. Writing gate
Before production lock, capture current lyric/version, structure, writer/contributor names, unresolved contribution questions, sample/interpolation concerns and Artist approval to progress.

## 4. Production gate
Maintain production brief: BPM, key, arrangement, sonic references, instrumentation/sound palette, producer/contributors, session version and outstanding decisions. Flag third-party material immediately.

## 5. Recording gate
Plan studio/session, engineer, performers, source requirements and objectives. After recording confirm session/source files, take notes, contributor details, backup and next comp/edit action.

## 6. Mix gate
Mix handoff must include approved production/session, consolidated brief, references, version naming and requested alternates. Feedback must be consolidated. Store approval status for every version.

## 7. Master gate
Mastering begins from the approved premaster. Track mastering engineer, date, versions, revision notes and technical delivery files. Final master requires explicit Artist approval.

## 8. Product QA
Before Release handoff verify:
- final approved master exists;
- file name/version is unambiguous;
- required alternates exist where applicable;
- lyrics are current;
- artist/title/version naming is approved;
- credits are captured;
- writer/split status is known or explicitly blocked;
- sample/interpolation/clearance status is known;
- metadata package is complete enough for distribution;
- catalog record links authoritative assets;
- artwork is handled by Content/Release but dependency is visible.

## 9. Metadata discipline
Never infer legal names, writer shares, publisher data, ISRC, UPC, ownership or clearance. Unknown is a valid state; fabricated certainty is not.

## 10. Version naming
Use deterministic names containing Song ID, title slug, stage, version/revision and date when useful. Never use ambiguous final_FINAL_v2 naming. The catalog record identifies the currently approved version.

## 11. Storage and backup
Preserve raw/source sessions, production sessions, consolidated stems, approved mixes, masters and relevant alternates. Maintain at least one separate backup location for critical masters/source files according to the artist's storage policy.

## 12. Release handoff
Release Agency receives: Song ID, approved master reference, title/version, artist/feature names, lyrics, explicit/language status, credits, writer/split status, rights/clearance status, ISRC status, catalog link, artwork dependency, release restrictions and Artist approval record.

## Definition of RELEASE_READY
A song is RELEASE_READY only when the approved master exists, required metadata is validated, material rights/clearance blockers are resolved or explicitly accepted by the authorized human/legal process, the catalog record is current and Release Agency has acknowledged the handoff.
