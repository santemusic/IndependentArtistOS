# PR & Media Operations

## Mission
Earn credible media attention, strengthen public understanding of the Artist and manage reputation-sensitive communication through truthful, relevant and disciplined media relations.

## Stage 0 — Intake
Required: Goal ID, Project/Release ID, objective, approved artist/release facts, release/event dates, priority territories, target audiences, approved positioning, available press assets, contact/spokesperson, embargo/exclusive status if any, sensitive-topic boundaries and approval owner. Missing critical inputs → NEEDS_INPUT.

## Stage 1 — Narrative architecture
Create: one-sentence story, short release angle, artist context, why-now rationale, verified proof points, secondary angles, local/cultural/technical angles where real, talking points, claims requiring verification and topics requiring escalation. Separate fact, artist opinion and PR framing.

## Stage 2 — EPK / press kit
Maintain current short/medium/long bio, release one-sheet, approved press photos, artwork, private/public listening links as appropriate, video, credits, verified selected achievements, existing coverage, social/site links and PR contact. Every item should have version/date and approval state.

## Stage 3 — Media research
Build targets by outlet type: music press, culture/lifestyle, local/regional, radio, podcasts, newsletters, blogs/trade and relevant niche media. For each target record outlet, person/role, recent relevant work, fit rationale, territory, priority, source/date verified, contact route and relationship history. Quality beats list size.

## Stage 4 — Pitch construction
A pitch should answer: Why this recipient? Why this Artist/release? Why now? What is the specific editorial angle? What proof/context supports it? What asset/access is available? What is the requested next action? Keep it concise. Personalize from actual research, not superficial mail-merge tokens.

## Stage 5 — Outreach
Prioritize high-fit targets. Track pitch version, date sent, channel, response, follow-up date and outcome. Respect opt-outs and reasonable follow-up cadence. Do not claim a send occurred without actual system confirmation. Commercial advertorial opportunities must be labeled separately from earned editorial.

## Stage 6 — Interview coordination
Capture outlet/interviewer, format, date/time/timezone, location/link, expected duration, recording/live status, topic, deadline, assets requested, logistics owner and publication expectation. Route calendar/logistics to Ops.

## Stage 7 — Interview preparation
Prepare outlet/interviewer context, strategic objective, likely questions, verified fact sheet, story bank, key messages, concise answers, difficult-topic bridges, topics requiring legal/human caution, pronunciation, CTA and interview-day checklist. Do not fabricate personal stories or train deception.

## Stage 8 — Coverage monitoring
Record confirmed coverage with source/link, date, outlet, author, type, key themes, notable quotes within lawful internal use, factual accuracy, sentiment classification with confidence, reach metrics only when sourced and follow-up opportunity. Feed relevant coverage IDs to Data/Growth/Content.

## Stage 9 — Corrections
Differentiate factual error from unfavorable opinion. For material factual errors: document exact claim, authoritative evidence, desired correction and contact history. Use respectful correction requests. Escalate legally significant false claims to Legal/human leadership.

## Stage 10 — Crisis protocol
Trigger for meaningful reputation, safety, legal or misinformation incidents. Immediately establish incident owner; known/unknown/alleged facts; source/timeline; stakeholder impact; urgency; legal/safety implications; monitoring plan; response options; approval chain. Default to accuracy over speed where facts are unclear. A holding statement may acknowledge awareness and fact-finding without speculation.

## Crisis approval gate
No external crisis statement, apology, accusation, admission, denial of disputed material facts or sensitive artist-personal disclosure may be published by an agent without explicit human approval and Legal review where legally material.

## Stage 11 — Post-campaign review
Measure targets researched, qualified pitches, responses, interviews, confirmed coverage, target quality, message pickup, referral/traffic signals where available, corrections, relationship gains and downstream campaign contribution. Do not use raw email volume as success.

## Cross-agency handoffs
Release → PR: dates, positioning, story, music/product facts and campaign milestones.
Content → PR: approved press photography, artwork/video and content assets.
PR → Content/Growth: confirmed coverage approved for amplification.
PR → Data: outreach/coverage identifiers and verified outcomes.
PR → Ops: interviews, deadlines and external commitments.
PR → Legal: material rights, defamation, contractual, privacy or crisis questions.

## Integrity
Never fabricate media interest, endorsements, reviews, quotes, numbers, achievements or exclusivity. Never disguise paid media as earned press. Never spam indiscriminately. Preserve journalist independence and distinguish editorial outcome from PR activity.
