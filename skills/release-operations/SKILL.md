# Release Operations

## Mission
Move approved music from RELEASE_READY to a coordinated, measurable market launch without losing control of assets, deadlines, rights, budget, approvals or learning.

## Stage 0 — Intake gate
Required: Goal ID, project ID, approved release candidate, target release date or window, artist approval state, primary objective, audience/territories, budget constraint, rights/splits status and accountable Release CEO. If mandatory information is unknown, mark NEEDS_INPUT.

## Stage 1 — Strategy
Define objective, audience, territories, positioning, release story, campaign phases, fan journey, primary CTA, KPI tree, budget assumptions, experiment backlog and risks. Strategy must be consistent with Artist/Brand direction.

## Stage 2 — Product readiness
Confirm Music Agency handoff: approved master, required audio variants, verified title/version, artist/feature names, lyrics, credits, contributor information, splits/rights status, artwork dependency, metadata record and source/archive links. Do not duplicate Music's ownership; validate the handoff.

## Stage 3 — Integrated back-plan
Back-plan from release date. Create deadlines for distribution delivery, artwork, content production, DSP pitching, pre-save/smart link, PR, creator seeding, CRM, paid tests, profile updates, release-day assets and analytics instrumentation. Identify critical path and hard external deadlines.

## Stage 4 — Distribution readiness
Validate audio file, artwork specification, metadata, release date, territories, artist mapping, version title, explicit/language fields, copyright lines, ISRC/UPC status and credits. Record actual submission confirmation. Verify ingestion and store mapping when observable. Never invent platform state.

## Stage 5 — DSP pitching
Prepare truthful platform-specific pitches using verified facts: song story, genre/context, contributors, priority territories, artist momentum and confirmed campaign support. Track submission deadline and actual outcome. Never promise placement or use fabricated achievements.

## Stage 6 — Pre-save / fan capture
Define landing/smart-link journey, CTA, destinations, attribution, consent-aware email/SMS capture where applicable and measurement plan. QA links across devices. Coordinate creative requirements with Content and promotion with Growth.

## Stage 7 — Content and campaign readiness
Request from Content: campaign world, hero assets, short-form batch, scripts/copy, artwork/social derivatives, platform variants and content calendar. Request from Growth: audience plan, organic distribution, CRM, creator seeding, paid tests and measurement. Request from PR: narrative, EPK and outreach where relevant.

## Stage 8 — T-minus readiness gates
T-8/T-6 weeks: master/artwork/distribution/content production underway.
T-6/T-4: distribution submitted, DSP pitch prepared, pre-save live, first teasers, PR/creator lists ready.
T-4/T-2: announcement/cadence, outreach, paid tests, community activation.
T-14 days: countdown, hook/CTA tests, profile/link QA, release-day schedule locked.
Adjust lead times to the actual project; never pretend a compressed timeline is risk-free.

## Stage 9 — Release-day command
Verify availability, correct audio/artwork/metadata, artist mapping and links. Coordinate approved website/link updates, launch content, fan communication, PR follow-up, creator activity, community coverage and paid activation. Maintain incident log. Critical distribution errors are P0.

## Stage 10 — Sustain
Use observed performance to select next content angles, live/lyric/BTS/story assets, creator follow-up, retargeting, PR re-pitch, playlist follow-up and budget changes. Scaling spend requires authorized Growth/Finance controls.

## Stage 11 — Measurement cadence
D1: availability, incidents, first signals.
D3: early creative/source/territory patterns.
D7: meaningful first-week comparison to plan.
D14: sustain effectiveness and audience conversion.
D28: campaign-level assessment.
Longer windows may be added based on goal.

## Stage 12 — Postmortem
Record goal vs result, what shipped, timeline failures, strongest/weakest assets, audience/territory findings, distribution/DSP outcomes, spend where available, fan conversion, PR/creator outcomes, operational failures, hypotheses, reusable wins, SOP changes and next-release decisions.

## Release readiness definition
A release is GREEN only when all mandatory critical-path inputs have evidence, responsible owners acknowledge their work, required human approvals are recorded and no unresolved blocker threatens launch. Unknown is not GREEN.

## Cross-agency handoffs
Music → Release: approved product package.
Release → Content: positioning, story, audience, CTA, deadlines.
Content → Growth: approved assets/copy/variants.
Growth → Data: campaign IDs, audiences, spend/objective/creative identifiers when available.
Data → Release/Executive: outcome, insight, confidence and recommended action.
Legal/Finance: review rights/commercial/budget matters within their authority.
