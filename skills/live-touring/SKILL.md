# Live & Touring Operations

## Mission
Convert live opportunities into strategically useful, safe, technically ready and financially understood performances with controlled handoffs from first opportunity through settlement and post-show learning.

## Stage 0 — Opportunity intake
Capture: Opportunity ID, Goal ID, promoter/buyer, venue/festival, city/country, proposed date, capacity, billing, set length, offer/deal structure, ticket context, curfew, exclusivity/radius clauses if known, travel implications, decision deadline and source evidence. Unknown terms remain UNKNOWN.

## Stage 1 — Opportunity evaluation
Evaluate strategic fit, market/audience fit, release-cycle timing, routing, fee/deal, expected costs, production feasibility, artist schedule/wellbeing impact, content/PR value and contractual risk. Produce ACCEPT / NEGOTIATE / HOLD / DECLINE recommendation; binding acceptance follows authority controls.

## Stage 2 — Booking pipeline
States: PROSPECT → CONTACTED → AVAIL_CHECK → HOLD → OFFER → NEGOTIATION → CONTRACT_REVIEW → CONFIRMED → COMPLETED / CANCELLED. Evidence is required to represent a hold, offer or confirmation as real.

## Stage 3 — Contract and budget gate
Route material deal terms to Legal and Finance before commitment. Capture guarantee/percentage, deposit/payment timing, expenses, cancellation, force majeure, radius/exclusivity, recording/broadcast, merch terms, guest list, hospitality, production obligations and settlement method as applicable. Never substitute this operational checklist for legal advice.

## Stage 4 — Routing and itinerary
For multiple shows, model travel time, border/visa/work-permit requirements where applicable, rest, freight/backline movement, hotel, ground transport, load-in and curfew constraints. Avoid impossible same-day assumptions. Sensitive itinerary information follows least-privilege access.

## Stage 5 — Advance
Create authoritative show record with venue/promoter/production contacts, addresses, access/load-in, parking, credentials, dressing rooms, catering, production specs, soundcheck, doors, set, curfew, guest process, merch, content access, settlement contact and emergency/escalation path. Track each item CONFIRMED / PENDING / CONFLICT / N/A.

## Stage 6 — Technical production
Maintain rider, stage plot, input list, backline, playback, PA/monitor/IEM requirements, microphones/DIs, lighting, power, stage dimensions and local crew requirements. Compare artist requirement to venue offer and log substitutions. Safety-critical decisions remain with qualified onsite professionals.

## Stage 7 — Rehearsal
Define setlist/show-flow version, live arrangements, transitions, playback/click/cues, instrument changes, talking points where desired and contingency paths. Rehearse highest-risk transitions and technical dependencies, then run the show end-to-end where practical. Record unresolved issues.

## Stage 8 — Hospitality / artist care
Advance contractual hospitality and operational artist-care requirements. Minimize sensitive-data exposure. Surface missing essentials early. Do not convert private preferences into broadly accessible documentation unnecessarily.

## Stage 9 — Promotion and fan journey
Coordinate with Growth/CRM/PR: announcement, ticket link, geo-targeted content, local media/creator opportunities, fan capture, guest/VIP mechanics where applicable and post-show retention. Live Agency supplies verified show facts; Growth owns campaign execution.

## Stage 10 — Show content
Create capture brief linked to campaign goals. Prioritize hero moments and reusable material. Confirm access/credential restrictions and known usage permissions. Capture should not interfere with safety or show execution. Register source assets with Content Factory after the show.

## Stage 11 — Show-day command
Use a day sheet. Verify arrival, venue access, production readiness, hospitality, credentials, merch, content crew, soundcheck, doors/set/curfew and settlement owner. Maintain incident/change log. Critical safety, unavailable production or contract-performance issues escalate immediately.

## Stage 12 — Settlement
Reconcile deal terms against promoter settlement. Record gross/ticket/attendance inputs where available, guarantee/percentage calculations, agreed deductions, bonuses, merch terms, commissions, expenses, tax/withholding inputs if supplied and payment status. Discrepancies remain OPEN until resolved. Finance receives the final evidence package.

## Stage 13 — Post-show review
Within the appropriate window capture attendance, ticket outcome, audience response, content captured, fan/CRM growth where measurable, press/creator outcomes, production issues, hospitality issues, travel/routing issues, settlement/payment status, show economics, market signal and recommended next action.

## Readiness gate
A show is GREEN only when mandatory contract/authority, travel, technical, schedule, staffing and venue-advance dependencies have evidence appropriate to the engagement. UNKNOWN critical-path items cannot be GREEN.

## Cross-agency handoffs
Executive/Release → Live: goal, timing and strategic priority.
Live → Legal: deal/contract and rights questions.
Live → Finance: budget, deposits, settlement and P&L inputs.
Live → Content: capture opportunities and source media.
Live → Growth/CRM: verified show facts, ticket destination and audience geography.
Live → PR: local press/interview opportunity.
Live → Data: show IDs, attendance/ticket/economic and campaign identifiers when available.

## Integrity and safety
Never invent confirmations, offers, ticket sales, attendance, payments, travel bookings or technical readiness. Do not expose private itineraries publicly. Do not override qualified onsite safety professionals. Material commitments remain subject to the defined human/legal/financial approval gates.
