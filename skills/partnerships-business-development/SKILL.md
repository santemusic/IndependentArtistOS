# Partnerships & Business Development

## Mission
Create high-fit, economically rational and brand-safe commercial opportunities from the Artist's music, audience, identity, live platform and cultural relevance, while preserving human control over commitments and rights.

## Stage 0 — Strategic guardrails
Maintain approved partnership thesis: desired categories, prohibited/conflicted categories, priority territories, audience truths, artist values/brand boundaries, acceptable opportunity types, current exclusivities, rights constraints and minimum strategic/commercial thresholds. Unknown restrictions must be checked before commitment.

## Stage 1 — Opportunity sourcing
Sources may include inbound requests, outbound prospecting, existing relationships, agencies, managers, labels/distributors, sync supervisors, publishers, creators, live promoters and referrals. Register source and evidence. Never fabricate interest.

## Stage 2 — Qualification
Score opportunity for artist/brand fit, audience relevance, creative potential, strategic value, economics, workload, rights/usage burden, exclusivity/conflict, reputation risk, timing and probability. Result: QUALIFY / NURTURE / DECLINE / NEEDS_INPUT.

## Stage 3 — Concept development
Build opportunity around mutual value. Define partner objective, artist value, audience value, core concept, possible deliverables, channels, timing, measurement and dependencies. Avoid generic logo-placement thinking when a culturally credible creative idea is possible.

## Stage 4 — Opportunity types
Brand partnership: endorsement, campaign, content, product, experience or co-creation.
Sponsorship: tour/show/event/content-series/platform inventory.
Sync/licensing: master/composition usage in film, TV, advertising, games, trailers or other media.
Collaboration: music, content, live, creator or strategic co-creation.
Strategic partnership: distribution, platform, technology, community or long-term ecosystem relationship.

## Stage 5 — Commercial scope
Capture fee/value, deliverables, quantity, channels, production responsibility, schedule, approval process, usage/media, term, territory, exclusivity/category, whitelisting/paid amplification, name/image/likeness usage, music rights, content ownership, edit rights, options, cancellation, expenses, reporting and payment timing as applicable. These are issue-spotting fields, not legal conclusions.

## Stage 6 — Negotiation support
Prepare BATNA/context where known, target outcome, priority issues, tradeable terms, non-negotiable/escalation points and scenario comparisons. Quantify hidden production/time/media costs. Legal interprets contracts/rights; Finance validates economics/tax/payment considerations; Artist/human authority approves binding commitments.

## Stage 7 — Sync workflow
Brief intake → catalog match → rights/control check → assets → pitch → interest → quote inputs → clearance/legal → license execution → asset delivery → usage verification → payment/royalty tracking handoff. Maintain instrumental/clean/stem readiness where available. Never assume one-stop control.

## Stage 8 — Collaboration workflow
Fit hypothesis → relationship/outreach → creative scope → credits/rights/economics → Music/Content production → Release/Growth plan → approvals → launch → results → relationship follow-up. Audience overlap is useful but creative reason comes first.

## Stage 9 — Contract/approval gate
Before SIGNED/ACTIVATION, verify the appropriate evidence for agreed scope, Legal review where required, Finance approval where required, Artist/human approval, rights/usage understanding, exclusivity/conflict check and accountable activation owner. An email conversation is not automatically a signed agreement.

## Stage 10 — Activation
Create deliverables matrix with item, owner, specification, due date, partner input, internal approval, partner approval, disclosure, usage status, publish/delivery state and proof of performance. Route production to Content, distribution to Growth/PR/Live/Release as appropriate and project control to Operations.

## Stage 11 — Measurement
Define partner-facing and internal metrics separately. Possible measures: delivered assets, reach/views, engagement, traffic, fan capture, sales/conversions, attendance, content reuse, press, brand-lift study supplied by partner, sync usage, revenue and relationship value. Do not guarantee outcomes not contractually controllable.

## Stage 12 — Financial close
Verify deliverables/proof required for invoice milestone. Hand contract/commercial terms and completion evidence to Finance. Track invoiced/paid state only from evidence. Flag late/disputed payment.

## Stage 13 — Postmortem / relationship
Record strategic result, economics, operational cost, creative quality, audience response, partner quality, scope creep, approval friction, rights complexity, measurement quality and next relationship action. Classify partner as EXPAND / MAINTAIN / NURTURE / AVOID with rationale.

## Deal pipeline
PROSPECT → QUALIFIED → CONTACTED → INTEREST → SCOPING → PROPOSAL → NEGOTIATION → LEGAL_REVIEW → APPROVAL → SIGNED → ACTIVATION → COMPLETE / LOST / DECLINED.
Each opportunity requires ID, owner, counterpart, type, source, value estimate, confidence, deadline, stage, blocker and next action.

## Cross-agency handoffs
Executive → Partnerships: strategic goals/guardrails.
Partnerships → Legal: contracts, rights, usage, exclusivity, disclosures.
Partnerships → Finance: economics, invoicing/payment, tax/accounting questions.
Partnerships → Content: approved creative deliverables.
Partnerships → Growth: approved distribution/amplification and tracking.
Partnerships → PR: public announcement/media opportunities.
Partnerships → Live: show/tour sponsorship activation.
Partnerships → Music/Release: collaboration/sync/release dependencies.
Partnerships → Data: campaign/deal identifiers and measurement plan.

## Integrity controls
Never fabricate partner interest, deal value, signed state, rights control, payment or campaign results. Never hide paid sponsorship where disclosure is required. Never bind the Artist or grant rights without authority. Never use inflated or invented audience data in sales materials.
