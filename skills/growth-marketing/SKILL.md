# Marketing & Growth Operations

## Mission
Grow qualified attention and convert it into durable fandom, owned relationships and measurable artist outcomes using ethical, evidence-driven acquisition and retention.

## Stage 0 — Intake
Required: Goal ID, Project/Release ID, campaign objective, baseline/target, audience, territories, campaign phase, approved positioning, publish-ready asset availability, CTA/fan destination, budget authority, measurement window and known constraints. Missing mandatory inputs → NEEDS_INPUT.

## Stage 1 — Audience architecture
Segment by relationship and behavior first: unaware/prospect, viewer/engager, follower, listener, saver/sharer, owned contact, buyer/attendee and superfan/advocate. Add territory, language, platform/context and relevant affinity only where useful and lawful. Avoid pretending demographic precision exists without evidence.

## Stage 2 — Funnel
Default conceptual funnel: IMPRESSION → QUALIFIED VIEW → ENGAGEMENT → PROFILE/VISIT → FOLLOW → OWNED CAPTURE → STREAM/LISTEN → SAVE/SHARE → PURCHASE/TICKET → REPEAT → ADVOCACY. Not every campaign needs every stage. Select the shortest path appropriate to the objective.

## Stage 3 — Channel roles
Organic social: discovery, identity, story, proof and recurring touchpoints.
Community: relationship depth, feedback, participation and advocacy.
CRM: owned relationship, lifecycle, launches and retention.
Creators: trusted distribution into relevant contexts and new audiences.
Paid: controlled amplification, creative/audience testing, retargeting and conversion.
Playlist: legitimate discovery through official editorial and credible curator ecosystems.
Landing/smart links: route intent and capture measurable actions.

## Stage 4 — Campaign brief
Define objective, primary KPI, target, measurement window, audience segments, messaging angles, channel roles, CTA, destination, creative requirements, budget by channel, experiment backlog, attribution assumptions, reporting cadence, stop rules and scale rules.

## Stage 5 — Organic social operations
Use Content Factory PUBLISH_READY assets. Maintain content ID, platform, copy, CTA, scheduled/published state and resulting post ID. QA links/tags/covers before publication. Optimize cadence from evidence and capacity, not generic frequency rules. Feed post identifiers to Data.

## Stage 6 — Community operations
Listen → classify → respond/route → capture insight → identify advocates → create participation opportunities → measure quality. Maintain boundaries for sensitive topics and crisis signals. Artist-personal replies require appropriate authorization; never impersonate private communication.

## Stage 7 — Paid media
For each campaign define objective, platform, audience, exclusions, placements, optimization event, creative IDs, landing destination, test budget, maximum approved spend, attribution window/assumption and decision rules. Monitor delivery, spend, CPM, view/attention metrics, CTR, CPC, landing conversion, downstream fan quality and frequency/fatigue as available. Stop tracking-broken or clearly low-quality campaigns. Scaling beyond authority requires approval.

## Stage 8 — CRM / owned audience
Capture only with valid consent. Define source, consent, territory where relevant, interests/behavior and lifecycle stage. Operate welcome, release, ticket, merch and retention messaging selectively. QA links and suppression/unsubscribe behavior. Prioritize click/conversion/repeat behavior over unreliable vanity signals.

## Stage 9 — Creator seeding
Build prospect list → fit score → personalized outreach → response → terms/permissions → asset delivery → posting status → content ID → results → relationship follow-up. Separate organic gifting, unpaid seeding and paid sponsorship. Coordinate commercial agreements with Partnerships/Finance/Legal as needed and surface disclosure requirements.

## Stage 10 — Playlist growth
Official DSP editorial pitching belongs with Release/DSP Pitch. Growth may run legitimate independent-curator discovery and outreach. Record fit, submission path, outcome and quality. Reject guaranteed streams, bot networks, fake engagement and undisclosed pay-for-play.

## Stage 11 — Experiment system
Each experiment requires: hypothesis, independent variable, audience/context, creative IDs, primary metric, guardrail metric, test duration/sample rationale, stop condition and decision rule. Avoid changing multiple variables when the goal is causal learning. Record inconclusive tests honestly.

## Stage 12 — Fan economics
Where data permits, track acquisition cost, owned-contact conversion, repeat engagement, ticket/merch conversion and cohort value. Do not manufacture LTV precision from insufficient data. Use ranges/assumptions explicitly.

## Stage 13 — Optimization
Classify results as SCALE, ITERATE, HOLD, STOP or INCONCLUSIVE. Scale only when the observed result aligns with the actual artist goal, downstream quality is acceptable and budget authority exists. A high CTR with poor fan quality is not necessarily a winner.

## Stage 14 — Reporting handoff
Growth → Data should include campaign/channel IDs, dates, objective, audiences, creative/content IDs, spend where applicable, destination, conversions/events, material changes and known tracking limitations. Data → Growth returns normalized results, uncertainty and recommended action.

## Integrity and platform safety
Never buy fake streams, followers, saves, comments or playlist placement. Never coordinate deceptive engagement or impersonation. Never use scraped/unauthorized personal data. Respect consent, unsubscribe and disclosure requirements. Do not claim organic success for paid activity. Never report unverified platform actions as completed.

## Human approval gates
Material budget increases, sensitive targeting/positioning, major paid partnerships, new personal-data use, crisis communications and other actions designated in system permissions require the appropriate human/CEO approval.
