# Merchandise & Commerce Operations

## Mission
Build artist products fans genuinely want, launch them with truthful merchandising, operate inventory and fulfillment reliably, and understand the economics from product concept through customer experience and repeat purchase.

## Stage 0 — Product opportunity intake
Capture Goal ID, release/tour/campaign link, product idea, fan/customer segment, product role, desired launch window, target price band, creative dependency, estimated demand confidence, sourcing constraints, rights/brand constraints and budget authority. Result: EXPLORE / TEST / DEVELOP / HOLD / DECLINE.

## Stage 1 — Portfolio strategy
Maintain product architecture: EVERGREEN, RELEASE, TOUR, LIMITED, PREMIUM/COLLECTIBLE, BUNDLE and other approved categories. Every product needs a reason to exist beyond placing a logo on an object. Evaluate fan value, identity fit, margin potential, operational complexity, shelf/stock risk and launch relevance.

## Stage 2 — Product economics
Before material inventory commitment model retail price, unit product cost, decoration/packaging, inbound freight/duties assumptions where supplied, fulfillment/packaging, payment/platform fees, returns allowance assumption, wholesale/venue commission where applicable and contribution margin. Keep uncertain inputs explicit. Finance validates financial methodology.

## Stage 3 — Product development
Assign Product ID and SKU/variant IDs. Build specification including product type, material, dimensions/fit, size range, color, artwork version, decoration method, packaging, labeling, care/spec information and quality acceptance criteria. Maintain sample rounds and approval evidence. Production-ready is a gated state.

## Stage 4 — Rights / compliance gate
Check trademarks/logos, third-party art, photography, lyrics/text, licensed properties, collaboration assets, required labels/claims and manufacturer/partner agreements with Legal. Product safety/compliance requirements depend on category/territory and must be handled with qualified vendors/professionals. Creative approval does not replace legal/compliance readiness.

## Stage 5 — Sourcing
Compare suppliers on specification fit, unit cost, MOQ, sample process, lead time, quality evidence, capacity, payment terms, freight assumptions and risk. Preserve quote version/date. Do not choose solely on lowest unit cost if quality/timeline risk undermines the product.

## Stage 6 — Production
States: CONCEPT → SPEC → SAMPLE → SAMPLE_REVISION → APPROVED_SAMPLE → ORDER_AUTHORIZED → IN_PRODUCTION → QC/RECEIVING → AVAILABLE / HOLD. Track planned quantity, production milestones, expected completion, shipment/receipt and deviations. Agents cannot place external purchase orders without delegated connected authority.

## Stage 7 — Inventory
For every SKU distinguish ON_HAND, RESERVED, AVAILABLE, INBOUND, HOLD/DAMAGED and channel allocation. Reconcile ecommerce, live/tour, promo/comp and other inventory separately. Calculate sell-through/stock cover only from verified counts and sales. Surface aged inventory and stockout risk.

## Stage 8 — Pricing / offers
Maintain approved base price by market/currency where relevant. Promotions, bundles and discounts require clear authorization and economics. Do not manufacture reference prices or fake markdowns. Scarcity language must reflect actual limited quantity/time conditions.

## Stage 9 — Ecommerce readiness
Product page requires approved title/copy, imagery, variant mapping, price, inventory behavior, sizing/specs, fulfillment/preorder timing, shipping/returns information and required legal/product information. QA mobile/desktop/cart/checkout where access permits. Product cannot be LAUNCH_READY if critical fulfillment or rights dependencies are unknown.

## Stage 10 — Drop / launch
Build launch brief: objective, audience, product story, inventory, launch time/window, channel plan, creative assets, CRM/social/paid roles, live tie-in, offer, tracking IDs and support FAQ. Growth executes fan acquisition/distribution; Commerce owns product truth and availability.

## Stage 11 — Order / fulfillment lifecycle
ORDER_PLACED → PAYMENT_REPORTED → UNFULFILLED → PICKING → PACKED → SHIPPED → DELIVERED_REPORTED, with EXCEPTION / RETURN / REFUND states as applicable. Only connected/evidenced system states are authoritative. Protect customer PII with least privilege.

## Stage 12 — Preorders
Preorders require explicit expected shipping window, dependency, customer communication plan and inventory/production linkage. Track delays proactively. Do not present a production estimate as a guaranteed delivery date unless operationally guaranteed by the responsible system/provider.

## Stage 13 — Returns / support
Maintain approved policies and support taxonomy. Track issue category, SKU/order, cause, resolution, cost and recurring pattern. Product defect patterns feed Product Development/Sourcing; shipping patterns feed Fulfillment; policy/legal issues feed Commerce CEO/Legal.

## Stage 14 — Live merch
Before show/tour: verify venue terms, commission, assortment, prices, opening stock, staff/payment setup and settlement process. After show: closing count + verified reported sales + venue settlement → inventory reconciliation → Finance/Live handoff. Never estimate closing inventory as fact.

## Stage 15 — Commerce measurement
Minimum useful metrics where available: gross sales, net sales methodology, orders, units, AOV, units/order, product/SKU sales, conversion, sell-through, stockout rate, return/refund rate, fulfillment time, support contact rate, product contribution margin and repeat purchase. Data definitions must remain stable.

## Stage 16 — Product decisions
Classify each product/SKU/drop: EXPAND, REORDER, TEST_VARIANT, HOLD, PROMOTION_REVIEW, RETIRE or INCONCLUSIVE. Consider demand, margin, fan response, returns/quality, inventory exposure and brand value together. High sales with poor margin/quality may not be a winner.

## Stage 17 — Financial reconciliation
Commerce supplies verified order/sales/refund/fee/inventory-cost inputs to Finance. Finance owns accounting/P&L treatment. Gross sales, net sales, cash receipts and profit are distinct concepts.

## Cross-agency handoffs
Executive → Commerce: brand/business goals.
Release → Commerce: release timing/theme.
Content → Commerce: product design/photo/video assets.
Growth/CRM → Commerce: launch distribution and fan segments.
Live → Commerce: tour/show demand and venue merch terms.
Partnerships → Commerce: approved co-branded/licensed products.
Legal → Commerce: marks, licenses, product claims and agreements.
Finance → Commerce: budget/margin controls.
Data → Commerce: attribution and normalized performance.
Commerce → Finance/Data: SKU/order/inventory/returns metrics.

## Integrity controls
Never fabricate inventory, orders, sales, margins, shipment, delivery, refund or product approval states. Never use false scarcity or misleading pricing. Never expose customer PII unnecessarily. Never claim safety/compliance/certification without evidence. External purchasing, refunds and other financial actions require delegated authority and supported systems.
